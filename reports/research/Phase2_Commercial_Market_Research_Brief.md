# Henkel APC Commercial Intelligence — Phase 2
## Market Demand, Application Value & Evidence-Based White-Space Research
**Research cutoff:** 11 November 2026  
**Project type:** Independently developed educational portfolio case; **not** an internal Henkel study.  
**Commercial perimeter:** Henkel Adhesive Technologies → Packaging & Consumer Goods (APC). The team's internal 'FSC' reporting definition has not been independently verified.

---

## Executive decision

**Question:** If the Adhesive Technologies business unit eventually experiences a lower pricing contribution to growth, which *qualified customer applications and channels* could generate profitable incremental volume in APC?

We assess two publicly documented APC application markets:

1. **Footwear / Sports & Fashion** — Where can additional qualified bonding applications and customers be found in concentrated manufacturing clusters?
2. **Packaging & Converting** — Can redesigned packaging, high-speed converting requirements and technical qualification lead to profitable commercial wins?

The decision is **not** to assert an imminent opportunity. It is to request the next evidence that would make one opportunity investable.

## 1. Footwear — what the market indicators actually support

The 2025 worldwide footwear manufacturing volume is broadly flat, even though the footprint is enormous. The concentration of output in Asian countries can help determine *where to investigate customers and production processes*, but it does not tell us:
- whether Henkel is already serving those customers;
- how many grams of adhesive each shoe uses;
- which applications are technically addressable;
- whether a manufacturer can change suppliers;
- the customer's realized adhesive price or expected contribution.

**Hypothesis F1:** A disciplined account/plant map for India and Vietnam might reveal qualified sales opportunities among manufacturers who lack the relevant Henkel solution. **Disconfirming test:** Most relevant plants are already covered, cannot qualify the technology, or have uneconomic economics.

**Hypothesis F2:** Henkel's footwear bonding options (PUR hot melts, one-side adhesives and water-based technologies) may enable *verified productivity, defect reduction or safety benefits*, generating value-led customer wins even when overall footwear production is flat. **Disconfirming test:** Controlled customer trials find no net cost-in-use improvement or product incompatibility. Source: https://next.henkel-adhesives.com/at/en/industries/sports-and-fashion/sports-shoes.html

**Hypothesis F3:** The selected-country Q1 2026 export contraction may indicate near-term demand sensitivity. Before attributing a distributor downturn to poor execution, compare the customer's production schedule, export exposure, backlog, sell-out and inventory.

**Useful commercial KPIs:** account penetration, qualified factory coverage, technical trial conversion, adhesive consumption per unit, weighted opportunity pipeline, gross contribution per account, and retention.

## 2. Packaging & Converting — the regulation/innovation pathway

Packaging adhesives serve applications including paper converting, corrugated board, folding cartons, end-of-line closure, flexible packaging and labels. Henkel publicly describes hot-melt and water-based systems and substrate-specific testing requirements. Sources:
- https://next.henkel-adhesives.com/us/en/industries/packaging-and-converting.html
- https://next.henkel-adhesives.com/us/en/industries/packaging-and-converting/paper-solutions.html

**Hypothesis P1:** PPWR-driven packaging redesign creates conversations in which a technical supplier may qualify new or improved adhesives. **Not proven:** EU regulation automatically increases Henkel's sales. Relevant dates and scope must be assessed product by product. Source: https://environment.ec.europa.eu/news/new-eu-rules-packaging-enter-application-2026-08-11_en

**Hypothesis P2:** Cost-in-use gains from better line stability, less waste and reliable bonding could support cross-selling to existing converters. **Disconfirming test:** Incumbent products already meet needs at a lower total cost.

**Hypothesis P3:** External material statistics help select research questions but not size the adhesive market. The 81.5 million tonnes of EU waste is a material-flow statistic. To build an adhesive SAM, a commercial team needs converter-level production, process type, adhesive dosing, average annual usage and obtainable customer coverage.

**Competitive note:** H.B. Fuller publicly lists paper converting adhesives and water-based barrier coatings. This establishes competing technologies but *does not* establish comparative market share, pricing or Henkel's relative sales performance. Source: https://www.hbfuller.com/en/industries/packaging

## 3. The correct commercial calculation

The workbook's six white-space scenarios are **fully hypothetical**. Account counts, technical-fit percentages, volumes, prices, gross margins, channel costs, win/capture assumptions and technical trials are **synthetic inputs** and cannot be presented as Henkel disclosures.

For each scenario:
1. **Eligible accounts** = assumed targeted accounts × assumed technical-fit rate.
2. **Addressable sales** = eligible accounts × annual adhesive kg per account × assumed EUR/kg.
3. **Capture-case revenue** = addressable sales × assumed capture percentage.
4. **Gross profit** = capture-case revenue × assumed gross margin.
5. **Partner costs** = capture-case revenue × assumed distribution cost percentage.
6. **Net contribution** = gross profit − partner costs − assumed technical-trial/onboarding costs.
7. **Break-even capture** = fixed trial cost ÷ [addressable sales × (gross margin − partner cost rate)].
8. **Demand stress test** = reduce captured sales by 20%, re-evaluate net contribution.

**Example (fictional EU paper converter case W04):** 70 possible accounts × 45% qualified × 15,000 kg/account × €2.80/kg × 25% capture = **€330,750 hypothetical annual sales**. At assumed gross margin 32%, partner costs 8% of sales and €35,000 trial costs, hypothetical net contribution is **€44,380**. This is **not** an estimated Henkel opportunity.

Across all six invented cases, workbook formulas return **€1,411,101 simulated annual captured revenue** and **€126,426 simulated net contribution**. These totals should not be used as APC sales forecasts. One case is already negative under the base assumptions, illustrating the importance of qualification economics.

## 4. What to validate before recommending any commercial investment

| Decision gate | Needed evidence | Why |
|---|---|---|
| Exact FSC team scope | Manager confirmation | Avoid wrong market / commercial perimeter |
| Distributor sell-in vs sell-out | Customer-level sell-out and inventory | Avoid mistaking inventory loading for end demand |
| Qualified target universe | CRM + plant list + actual supplier footprint | Production totals are not available customers |
| Product technical compatibility | Engineer-verified material and line trials | Product fit is often binary |
| Customer cost-in-use | Yield, process time, waste and total cost | Differentiation must create value |
| Profitability | Real authorized pricing, costs, discounts and trial resources | Incremental revenue is not necessarily profit |
| PPWR relevance | Correct article, product and implementation date | 2026 and 2030 requirements differ |
| Competitor comparison | Objective testing, not vendor marketing | Avoid unsupported competitive claims |

## 5. Decision structure for a Sales & Channel Excellence team

**Footwear choice:** Account-cluster and technical application mapping is a testable route to identify potential customer wins, but global 2025 production growth was almost flat, and selected Q1 2026 exports weakened. Prioritize **quality of application fit and achievable share-of-wallet**, not just the size of the country's footwear output.

**Packaging choice:** Technical qualification and conversion-efficiency initiatives may become relevant as material systems change. PPWR is an external catalyst for conversations, not proof of future orders. Prioritize converters with documented redesign projects and credible qualification timelines.

**Cross-cutting recommendation:** Build a stage-gated commercial funnel: *market signal → named customer → validated application → feasible technical trial → cost-in-use proof → actual contribution estimate → sales action*. Do not convert a proxy into a financial forecast.

## 6. Interview-ready explanation

> “After reviewing Henkel's Q3 results, I wanted to investigate how a commercial team could build a pipeline of profitable volume growth beyond pricing. I focused on two application markets relevant to APC: footwear and packaging converting. I first established the public market signals and their limits. Then I mapped adhesives to customer manufacturing decisions, designed falsifiable hypotheses, and built a separate simulated opportunity model incorporating qualification, channel and implementation costs. The model doesn't claim to reveal Henkel's actual distributors or market share; instead, it demonstrates the evidence I would need to prioritize account development.”

## 7. Recommended next work package (Phase 3)

**Phase 3: Market-segment scorecards and customer validation plan.**

1. Select **one footwear cluster** (India or Vietnam) and **one EU packaging sub-application** (paper converting or flexible packaging).
2. Build a **public company/plant shortlist** based on documented manufacturers—not invented accounts—with source URLs and factory application descriptions.
3. Define what would qualify a plant as addressable (process, materials, adhesive application and procurement authority).
4. Build a mock discovery-interview guide for sales, engineering, distributor and buyer roles.
5. Replace only model assumptions for which a source is available; otherwise retain **SIMULATED** labels.

**Research standard:** No claims that these are actual Henkel customers or that these sites are uncovered without internal evidence. Public manufacturers can serve as *research candidates* only.
