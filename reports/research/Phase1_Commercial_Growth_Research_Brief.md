# Henkel Adhesive Technologies / APC
## Phase 1: Commercial Growth Quality and Sustainability Research
**Research cutoff:** 11 November 2026  
**Prepared for:** B2B Sales and Channel Excellence portfolio learning  
**Evidence standard:** Official Henkel investor financial statements and annual/half-year reports; hypothetical analytical scenarios explicitly segregated.

### Executive conclusion

Adhesive Technologies' Q3 2026 organic sales growth was **+7.9%**, composed of **+7.2 percentage points price** and **+0.7 percentage points volume**. The underlying reporting pattern is markedly different from Q3 2024 (price -0.2 pp / volume +3.9 pp; organic +3.7%) and Q3 2025 (price +0.5 pp / volume +2.0 pp; organic +2.5%). The contribution from price accounted for approximately 91% of the rounded Q3 2026 organic-growth figure.

**Interpretation:** There is a clear change in reported growth composition, **not proof** that growth is unhealthy or unsustainable. High pricing contribution could represent justified cost recovery, differentiated value or contract repricing. Henkel's Q3 report specifically referred to higher raw-material cost pressure when discussing its earnings outlook. Profitability and demand resilience therefore need to be assessed alongside price and volume.

**Important counterbalance:** The more representative **Jan–Sep 2026** Adhesive Technologies organic growth was **+5.7%**, comprising **+3.6 pp price** and **+2.1 pp volume**; in **Q2 2026**, the price/volume split was **+3.2 pp / +4.2 pp**. Growth composition can differ materially between quarter and cumulative reporting periods.

### 3. Why the volume contribution matters

1. A company can generate robust organic sales growth while customers purchase only marginally more volume; reported pricing may dominate.
2. A high pricing contribution may be sustainable when products provide value (e.g., manufacturing uptime, bonding quality, yield, material compatibility) or recover rising input costs.
3. Low positive volume contribution alone cannot establish weaker end-market demand, customer churn, physical tonnes sold, market share loss or channel failure.
4. The business research must distinguish **end-market demand** (manufacturing activity), **company competitiveness** (value and technical fit), and **commercial execution** (coverage and account performance).

### 4. Research hypotheses and evidence required

| Hypothesis | How to test | Data limits |
|---|---|---|
| Price increases reflect input-cost recovery | Compare Henkel raw-material commentary, producer prices, company adjusted margins | Customer-level net pricing unavailable |
| Customers will resist further price increases | Examine volume, customer retention, product qualification and competitor behavior | Price elasticity cannot be inferred from aggregate quarterly figures |
| Volume growth has latent channel opportunity | Analyze customer coverage, win/loss data, cross-selling, distributor sell-out and penetration | Internal data required; **only simulated examples available here** |
| APC growth reflects broad-based consumer-industry demand | Compare packaging and hygiene production conditions with Henkel commentary | No disclosed APC price/volume or customer-level revenue |
| Forward buying distorted some 2026 sales | Compare Q2–Q3 reports, inventory and customer order timing | Henkel H1 report states forward buying contributed to APC; effect size not disclosed |

The official H1 2026 financial report explicitly attributes part of APC's strong organic development to *customer forward buying in response to geopolitical uncertainties*. This is an additional reason not to extrapolate any single quarterly figure mechanically.

### 5. Scenario methodology

For a **transparent, hypothetical** target of +7.9 percentage points of annual organic-growth contribution, assume future price contribution normalizes to +2.0 pp:

- Target organic growth: +7.9%
- Assumed price contribution: +2.0 pp
- **Required volume contribution under additive pp bridge: +5.9 pp** (`7.9 − 2.0`)
- Incremental volume contribution relative to observed Q3 2026: **+5.2 pp** (`5.9 − 0.7`)

This is **not** a Henkel forecast, product-volume growth forecast or assertion that this volume is available. It identifies a commercial question that management would need to test: where could higher demand, better market coverage, acquisition, retention or cross-selling contribute profitable volume?

`P1_Scenarios` contains editable assumptions and formulas, with all nonobserved scenarios marked hypothetical.

### 6. Business and Channel Excellence implications

**Workstream A — Profitability first:** Before recommending aggressive volume expansion or discounting, assess contribution margin by product/customer, manufacturing value, cost pressure, and expected cost of sales support.

**Workstream B — Demand diagnostics:** Distinguish customer manufacturing output changes from supplier switching, lost orders, supply constraints, and seasonal or inventory timing effects.

**Workstream C — Channel efficiency:** Evaluate sell-in versus sell-out, account penetration, training effectiveness, overdue quotations, customer conversion and regional coverage. In this portfolio the channel dataset is **SIMULATED – NOT HENKEL DATA**; it is a method demonstration only.

**Workstream D — White-space potential:** Assess addressable manufacturer counts, adhesive spend, qualified applications, competitor presence, customer switching barriers and viable margins. Market size estimates require external evidence; never present unvalidated revenue opportunities as known Henkel white space.

**Workstream E — Decision and action design:** Prioritize account-acquisition campaigns, product cross-sell, technical qualification trials and distributor enablement by expected contribution, feasibility, confidence and time to result.

### 8. Questions for a global sales/channel excellence team

- Which customers and product families show strong volumes despite pricing increases?
- Is sell-in to distributors aligned with sell-out to end manufacturers, or is inventory timing influencing results?
- What percentage of volume risk is driven by low manufacturing demand versus lost specifications or customer switching?
- Which applications generate the strongest customer cost-in-use benefits and contribution margins?
- Which underpenetrated segments could provide profitable growth without price concessions?
- How is commercial opportunity quality measured and which KPIs trigger partner enablement?
