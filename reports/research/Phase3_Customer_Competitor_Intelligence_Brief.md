# Henkel APC Commercial Intelligence — Phase 3
## Customer, Competitor & Commercial Qualification Research | 11 November 2026

**Research objective.** Translate Henkel Adhesive Technologies' reported growth pattern and APC product applications into a defensible, evidence-led investigation of potential industrial customer environments. This is a public-data **research exercise**, not a Henkel CRM export, verified customer pipeline, proof of current supplier relationships, or company sales forecast.

### 1. Executive finding
Seven real manufacturing research units were documented from corporate sources: three footwear manufacturing organisations/networks in Vietnam and four corrugated-packaging sites in Germany and Poland. We identified three named potential sources of product competition from their official offering descriptions (H.B. Fuller, Bostik and Jowat; overlap depends on application), plus Henkel's own technology pages. Every named account's Henkel relationship, incumbent adhesive supplier, procurement authority, actual adhesive use, and available commercial value is **UNKNOWN**. No account is qualified for commercial outreach by this public research.

**The key analytical distinction:** a producer's manufacturing activity is a verified fact; the use of a particular supplier's adhesive is not. A product category fitting a manufacturing process suggests a technical question, not a contract, switching opportunity, or addressable revenue amount.

### 2. Footwear market — Vietnam cluster
**Verified manufacturers:** TBS Group manufactures and exports shoes from Vietnamese facilities; Pou Chen Group reports Vietnam footwear production bases including Dong Nai and other locations; TKG Taekwang's corporate page lists TKG Taekwang Vina in Bien Hoa/Dong Nai as a shoe producer. (S01–S03.)

**Henkel application research:** Henkel describes polyurethane hot melts, one-side adhesive approaches, and water-based footwear solutions for sports shoes. These technologies can create hypotheses about manufacturing speed, solvent exposure, durability and total cost-in-use, but the Henkel website does not establish that any of our three named manufacturers use these products. (S07.)

**Competitor landscape:** H.B. Fuller and Bostik both publish footwear adhesive offerings. The pages confirm product overlap but **not** account-level incumbent suppliers, prices, market shares, performance superiority or customer wins. (S10–S11.)

**Commercial case question:** Could a particular footwear line reduce total manufacturing cost through a change in bonding process?
1. Confirm which shoes, substrates and line speeds apply at the specific factory.
2. Identify the spec owner: global footwear brand, OEM engineering, or plant procurement.
3. Obtain the existing process baseline: adhesive kg per pair, scrap rate, labour, energy, downtime and approval conditions.
4. Invite engineering and product teams to define a permitted comparative trial.
5. Calculate incremental contribution net of material, trial, support and logistics costs.
6. Recommend action only if the technical gate and economics are both validated.

**What NOT to claim:** TBS/Pou Chen/TKG are not confirmed Henkel prospects or competitor customers; none of their industrial adhesive volumes or purchasing values are known from the cited pages.

### 3. Corrugated packaging — Germany and Poland
**Verified production sites:** Mondi Eschenbach in Germany describes corrugating, printing, finishing and glued e-commerce boxes (S04). Mondi's Mszczonow/Warsaw corrugated plant completed a €90 million expansion project in 2024, described as doubling capacity (S05). Saica confirms it acquired corrugated plants in Bydgoszcz and Wroclaw, Poland, in 2024 (S06). Avoid applying Saica's *total acquired-network capacity* to either site.

**Henkel technical applicability:** Its corrugated-board offer includes AQUENCE HALO corrugating additives, water-resistant solutions and converting technologies (S08–S09). For Eschenbach, the main business distinction to learn is **starch/corrugator additives versus downstream box closing / hot-melt or water-based converting**. These are different process applications with different selection criteria and potential economic benefits.

**Market/regulatory context:** The EU Packaging and Packaging Waste Regulation applies from 12 August 2026 (S14). Compliance can motivate design and material conversations, but it does not automatically require an adhesive swap, establish that a converter has a problem, or imply that Henkel owns the solution.

**Competitor landscape:** H.B. Fuller and Jowat describe offerings in packaging and paper adhesives (S12–S13). No published source here establishes a competitive advantage for a specific factory.

**Commercial case question:** Is a technical process-improvement trial commercially sensible for a particular corrugated production line?
- Identify the stage (corrugator adhesive / laminating / folding-carton sealing / e-commerce closure).
- Measure runnability, speed, waste, moisture performance, machine cleaning and food-contact requirements where relevant.
- Identify which materials and operating parameters are used.
- Calculate the avoided operating costs *after* trial/qualification and price differences.
- Confirm whether purchasing authority belongs to plant management or central procurement.

### 4. What the account research score means
The `P3_Account_Screening` sheet computes a **research-interest score**, not conversion probability or commercial attractiveness. Formula:
`Research interest = 20 × (0.40 × application relevance + 0.35 × source specificity + 0.25 × verified trigger)`.

Each input (0–5) is an analyst judgment based on *public information*. The score cannot answer whether the account is currently buying from Henkel, whether a competitor is incumbent, whether it wants to switch, or whether an opportunity has positive margins. Such unknowns are deliberately preserved as `Unknown` or `Unverified`.

### 5. Commercial qualification gates — what to validate before pitching
Each of the seven research units has seven unverified gates in `P3_Qualification_Gates`:
- Site/process and product-line verification
- Actual adhesive chemistry, materials and performance criteria
- Current supplier and any existing Henkel relationship
- Procurement/specification decision rights
- Technical trial and qualification feasibility
- Cost-in-use and contribution economics
- Channel/territory/service/compliance feasibility

A research case becomes a commercial opportunity only after authorized evidence supports these gates.

### 6. Simulated scenario lab — not named-account estimates
`P3_Scenario_Lab` creates four *anonymous*, hypothetical scenarios for feasibility learning. It calculates:
- `eligible_sites = assumed_sites × assumed_eligible_share`
- `annual_pool = eligible_sites × assumed_kg_per_site × assumed_EUR_per_kg`
- `captured_revenue = annual_pool × assumed_capture_rate`
- `net_contribution = captured_revenue × assumed_contribution_margin − assumed_trial_cost − assumed_support_cost`
- `break_even_capture_rate = (trial + support) / (annual_pool × margin)`
- `20% volume downside = 0.8 × captured_revenue × margin − trial − support`

These figures are **illustrations only**: not forecasts, customer spend, competitor share, or Henkel revenue.

### 7. Strategic learning: connecting to your earlier phases
- **Phase 1 financial signal:** Growth quality, price-volume mix and disclosed APC business-area growth.
- **Phase 2 market analysis:** Demand drivers, paper packaging compliance, product applications and opportunity scenario methods.
- **Phase 3 research:** What manufacturing activities are observed, which technical applications are plausible, and what commercial questions remain unverified.

If public Adhesive Technologies growth remains pricing-heavy, the relevant Sales Excellence decision is not automatically to lower prices or chase volume. It is to establish *where incremental, profitable volume can be won*, at what qualification cost and through which route to market.

### 8. Validation roadmap
1. **Manufacturing footprint:** Document the operating activities and applications of one Vietnam footwear site and one EU corrugated site.
2. **Application requirements:** Establish substrate, process, performance, qualification and approval requirements without inferring supplier relationships.
3. **Economic feasibility:** Evaluate technical trials, total cost in use, contribution margin and risk using transparent hypothetical inputs.
4. **Decision review:** Classify each hypothesis as supported, contradicted or unresolved and identify the missing evidence for a viable commercial opportunity.

### 9. Decision framework
Publicly identified manufacturers are research cases, not established customers or qualified leads. The recommended progression is verified manufacturing activity → technical relevance → decision authority → feasibility → economic value → commercial authorization. All unverified attributes remain explicitly unknown.
