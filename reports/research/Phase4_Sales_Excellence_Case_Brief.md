# Henkel Adhesive APC: Phase 4 Sales Diagnostics, Win/Loss & Commercial Decisions

**Prepared:** 11 November 2026  
**Purpose:** Learning exercise for global B2B Sales & Channel Excellence roles.  
**Evidence boundary:** Official Henkel sources establish product and company context. ALL Phase 4 accounts, orders, costs, CRM entries and commercial opportunities are fictional and simulated.

## Executive decision
Do **not** respond to a regional sales decline with immediate blanket discounts. Diagnose price, quantity, demand, channel execution, supply reliability and technical qualification before acting. Test a small set of interventions, using expected incremental contribution and explicit approval gates. Keep an option to do nothing until the evidence improves.

## 2. Synthetic assignment and method
We create eight generic customers (four Vietnamese footwear sites, four European paper-packaging converters) with 144 scenario monthly sales records for Jan–Sep 2025 and Jan–Sep 2026. There are 16 simulated CRM opportunities and 17 candidate actions. Distributors have clearly invented `SIM-DIST-*` IDs. These cases are not associated with named manufacturers from Phase 3.

For an illustrative account with Q0 = 14,000 kg at P0 = €4.20/kg and Q1 = 9,800 kg at P1 = €4.35/kg:
- Revenue before: 14,000 × €4.20 = **€58,800**.
- Revenue after: 9,800 × €4.35 = **€42,630**.
- Volume effect: (9,800 − 14,000) × €4.20 = **−€17,640**.
- Realized price effect: 9,800 × (€4.35 − €4.20) = **+€1,470**.
- Net sales change: **−€16,170**. The arithmetic reconciles exactly.

The price effect does not prove price rises caused the lost volume. Our synthetic CRM note suggests a competing technical specification; a customer interview, qualified bid data and an analysis of customer production would be necessary to distinguish lost share from weaker market demand.

**Interpretation:** Higher realized prices can mitigate an underlying volume contraction. We should not infer that discounting would fix it. In this exercise, causes differ by case: potential competitive displacement, end-market production slowdown, qualification delays, delivery performance and new-application growth. These are defined hypotheses and fictional scenario observations, NOT real Henkel diagnoses.

## 4. Win/loss analysis logic
1. Define what constitutes a real opportunity (customer need, technical fit, buyer, estimated volume and closing event).
2. Capture every Won/Lost/Open event, including a dated recorded reason and who supplied it.
3. Calculate win rate only on closed opportunity outcomes. Here: 5/(5+5) = **50%**, not 5/16.
4. Break down outcomes by application, region, bid size and sales stage.
5. Validate reasons before deciding on price, technical support or route-to-market changes. Quoted price objections could conceal switching costs, technical fit or procurement timing.
6. Measure the share of potential volume represented by the outcome, rather than using count alone, when relevant.

## 5. Commercial choices and economics
For each proposal, the model estimates incremental kg if an intervention succeeds, a subjective probability of success, net price per kg, variable cost, distributor/channel fee, trial cost, sales enablement cost and any retained-customer discount impact.

**Expected net contribution = incremental kg × (price − variable cost − channel fee) × success probability − trial cost − enablement cost − retained-base discount leakage.**

This is a scenario screening formula, not adjusted EBIT, not an investment NPV, and not a statistically estimated causal treatment effect. The break-even success probability tells the analyst how credible the action must be to avoid a modeled loss.

Selected examples from simulated scenarios:
- **SIM-VN-FW-01:** Technical qualification pilot, screened expected net contribution **€2,575**. Validate the target specification and actual buying criteria first.
- **SIM-VN-FW-04:** Complete the delayed qualification, **€2,292**. Stop if bond-performance criteria are not met.
- **SIM-EU-PK-01:** Repair order fulfillment/OTIF, **€1,995**. Verify whether better delivery changes the customer's order allocation.
- **SIM-EU-PK-02:** Scale paper-mailer application only after repeatable technical qualification, **€1,008**.
- **SIM-VN-FW-02:** HOLD. A production slowdown does not justify assuming more discounts will create end-customer demand.

The **€8,102** screened aggregate of selected modeled actions is NOT a company forecast. Actions may compete for scarce technical resources, their assumptions are not calibrated and some outcomes overlap. Management needs to verify economics and capacity before combining them.

## 6. Suggested discussion with a Sales Excellence manager
- How does the team distinguish an end-market contraction from customer-share loss in distributor sell-out data?
- What evidence is required before a CRM 'Lost: Price' code is accepted as the root cause?
- How do technical qualification steps affect opportunity aging and forecasting?
- Which leading indicators (OTIF, repeat order rate, trial conversion, active customers, share of wallet) are used alongside revenue?
- How does the team quantify intervention economics when price, service, channel margins and technical resources interact?
- How are market, account and distributor-level commercial data governed and reconciled across global regions?

## 7. What this project does not demonstrate
This research does **not** establish Henkel's actual customer losses, its real product-margin structure, distributor identities, internal FSC responsibilities, channel agreements, confidential contract prices, or causal effectiveness of any action. The next real-world step would require authorized CRM, ERP, sell-out, technical approval and customer feedback data, subject to Henkel permissions.

## 8. Future Phase 5
Build a responsible *commercial experimentation and management decision system*: account discovery interviews; pilot hypotheses; agreed KPIs and success thresholds; cost and capability constraints; structured pilots with pre/post or comparison-group analysis; and an executive decision memo presenting evidence, risks and next actions.
